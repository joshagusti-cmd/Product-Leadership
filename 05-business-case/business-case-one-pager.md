# Module 5: Business Case One-Pager

**Meridian: The Field Gap | Rock #1: Simplify daily logs from 14 required fields to four**  
**Decision requested:** Approve a $10,000 pilot within a $40,000 total build budget; release the remaining $30,000 only if the pilot passes. Allow $12,000 annual operating cost after rollout.

*Hypothetical coursework model, September 28, 2026. The Rock and strategic targets come from Modules 1–2. All financial inputs, cohort sizes, retention estimates, and pilot thresholds below are proposed assumptions, not observed results. This is separate from the AI Bid Estimation evaluation.*

## 1. The strategic bet

Make daily documentation easier for foremen and superintendents by reducing required fields from 14 to four while preserving enterprise records and controls. Less effort should increase regular field logging, give project managers more complete records, and strengthen the value customers see at renewal. The financial bet is **incremental retention of existing contracts**, not new subscriptions: across 100 eligible accounts, improve annual renewal from an assumed 85% to 90%, retaining five additional accounts. This supports the broader weekly field-adoption target of 18% to 60%; the single Rock is not credited with achieving that entire target.

## 2. The assumptions, ranked

1. **Adoption causes a five-percentage-point retention lift.** This changes the funding conclusion most. At a three-point lift, annual contribution after operating costs is $45,600; at two points, it is $26,400, below the $40,000 build cost. Faster logging alone is not proof of retention.
2. **There are 100 eligible accounts at $24,000 annual contract value and 80% gross margin.** Accounts must have renewal exposure within the measurement year. Count only retention above a comparable baseline, excluding unrelated sales interventions.
3. **Four required fields remove a meaningful adoption barrier.** An eight-week pilot across 20 accounts, split evenly between treatment and comparison, should produce at least a 15-percentage-point improvement in weekly active field users relative to the comparison group's change. Trust, connectivity, and response delays may still limit adoption.
4. **Delivery stays within $40,000 plus $12,000 annual operating cost.** The pilot is included in the build budget. Incremental operating cost is additional to the costs already reflected in the 80% margin.

## 3. The expected return

| Measure | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Per additional retained account | $24,000 retained annual revenue; $19,200 gross contribution |
| At scale | 100 × (90% − 85%) = **five additional retained accounts** |
| Annual benefit | **$120,000 retained revenue; $96,000 gross contribution** |
| After annual operating cost | $96,000 − $12,000 = **$84,000 contribution** |
| First full benefit-year net after build | $84,000 − $40,000 = **$44,000** |
| Break-even volume | Ceiling of ($40,000 + $12,000) ÷ $19,200 = **three additional retained accounts** |

Validate adoption by week eight; measure renewal impact over the 12 months following rollout. The $44,000 assumes a full year's revenue from all five saved contracts. Staggered renewals can reduce revenue recognized in that measurement window; this is not a launch-date cash-payback forecast. The pilot tests adoption feasibility, not statistically proven retention causality.

## 4. The kill criterion

**At the end of week eight**, if the treatment cohort's improvement in weekly active field users is **less than 15 percentage points above the comparison cohort's improvement**, the product lead stops expansion, cancels the unspent **$30,000** rollout allocation, avoids the planned **$12,000 annual operating commitment**, and reallocates capacity to the next approved field-adoption Rock. The maximum sunk pilot budget is **$10,000**. Define active usage as at least one logged item per licensed field user per week; freeze cohort definitions before starting.

**Twelve months after rollout**, if the estimated incremental retention benefit is **fewer than three accounts across the 100-account cohort**, stop further investment and reallocate the next year's $12,000 operating allocation where contractually avoidable, retaining essential customer support. The product lead and finance owner apply the pre-agreed comparison method. Passing the adoption gate authorizes rollout, not a claim that the financial return is proven.
