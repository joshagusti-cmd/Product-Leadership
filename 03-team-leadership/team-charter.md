# Module 3: Meridian team charter

Status: Drafted on 2026-09-21 for the hypothetical certification case. Proposed team agreements, not evidence of implementation. Q3 targets are inherited from the case strategy, not a new real-world delivery commitment.

## 1. What We Own

We own the field experience for foremen and superintendents, from capturing a jobsite issue to routing it and showing its resolution status. Our priorities are simplified daily logs, offline capture, and photo annotation. Redesigning the enterprise console, executive dashboards, and automated compliance tools is outside our current scope. Changes affecting shared systems require agreement with the owning team before implementation.

## 2. How We Decide

The product manager prioritizes work within the agreed field-adoption strategy. Engineering owns technical implementation decisions, and design owns interaction design, with the team reviewing customer and delivery implications together. The product lead approves changes to quarterly priorities.

Requests to reopen a priority must include new evidence, the customer or commercial impact, and what existing work would be displaced. Urgent renewal risks receive a review with the product lead, account owner, and engineering lead within two business days. We record the decision and rationale before changing sprint scope.

## 3. What Success Looks Like

By the end of Q3, increase weekly active field users from 18% to 60% of licensed field seats, increase field-originated issues resolved within the same-shift SLA from 34% to 70%, and reduce incidents first reported through informal channels from 65% to below 20%.

We review these measures weekly alongside field feedback to understand whether friction, trust, or response delays are limiting adoption. If a measure remains flat or worsens for three consecutive weeks, we review the evidence and adjust the approach.

## 4. How We Work

We raise blockers within one business day and assign an owner and next action. We challenge proposals directly and respectfully, using customer evidence and explicit trade-offs. Once a decision is recorded, we follow it unless new evidence warrants another review.

Sprint planning focuses on delivering agreed priorities; broader priority disputes go through the decision process above. We seek feedback from foremen or superintendents every sprint and close each retrospective with one improvement, a named owner, and a follow-up date.
