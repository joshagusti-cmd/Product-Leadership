# Module 2 - Backlog Audit: Rocks and Hard Nos (Meridian)

_Completed in the Module 2 Lab Guide (Exercise 1: Audit and Prioritize Your Backlog), reviewed and edited, then committed here. Scored against the Meridian Foundations 14-item backlog using the Impact vs. Effort matrix, north star = field adoption._

## Rocks

**Rock #1 - Backlog item #4: Simplify the daily log to 4 required fields (from 14)**
Source: foreman feedback. Removes the single biggest completion barrier for foremen and moves KR1 (weekly active field users, 18% to 60%) and KR3 (get incidents out of group texts) fastest, for the lowest effort in the backlog.

**Rock #2 - Backlog item #1: Build an offline-first mobile experience for job sites with poor connectivity**
Source: field research. Table stakes for the camera-first capture flow in our How to Win: none of our speed or trust work matters if the app can't log anything with no signal on site. Directly protects KR1 and KR2 (same-shift SLA resolution).

**Rock #3 - Backlog item #2: Add a photo markup and annotation tool for field teams**
Source: field superintendent interviews. This is the camera-first capture experience from our Capabilities Required section, the direct, in-app substitute for the group-text photo habit we're trying to displace, moving KR3 most directly.

## Hard Nos

**Hard No #1 - Backlog item #10: Build an executive reporting dashboard with custom KPIs**
Source: 2 renewals at risk. Two renewals are genuinely at risk, but a dashboard doesn't move field adoption; this is an account-management problem, not a roadmap item, and building it now would pull capacity straight off the Rocks above.

**Hard No #2 - Backlog item #5: Build an automated compliance checklist generator**
Source: enterprise sales, 3 clients. Requested by enterprise sales for 3 clients, but it deepens the enterprise-console complexity that's already driving field teams away, the opposite direction from our strategy.

**Hard No #3 - Backlog item #13: Refresh the web UI**
Source: sales team ("looks outdated" from prospects). This is a sales objection about the PM console, not a field-adoption problem; it's real feedback, but it doesn't touch a single foreman or superintendent.

## Show and swap (peer review)

**Do the Rock selections feel traceable to a clear strategy, or do they read like a feature list?**
My partner read all three Rocks as traceable to the field-adoption OKR without needing explanation; he said all three do the same job: making capture faster and possible everywhere on site. Her one push: she couldn't tell from the Rocks alone whether we're also solving the "why bother logging it here" trust question, or only the speed problem.

**Pick one Hard No and make the case for why it should actually be a Rock.**
He picked Hard No #1 (executive reporting dashboard) and argued it should be a Rock because two renewals are actively at risk; if we lose those accounts, we lose the field seats we're trying to convert in the first place, so the dashboard indirectly protects the OKR's denominator.
